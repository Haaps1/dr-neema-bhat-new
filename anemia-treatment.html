Anemia Treatment in Bangalore

Anemia means your blood has fewer healthy red blood cells or less hemoglobin than it needs, so less oxygen reaches your body and you feel tired, weak or breathless. Most anemia is treatable once the cause is found — and that is where the right anemia treatment in Bangalore begins.

Overview

What is anemia?

Your red blood cells carry oxygen from your lungs to every part of your body. They use a protein called hemoglobin to do this. When hemoglobin drops below the healthy level, your organs get less oxygen. This is anemia.

Anemia is very common in our state. About 48 out of every 100 women and two out of three young children in Karnataka have anemia (NFHS-5). Many people live with it for years, thinking tiredness is “normal”. It is not, and with timely anemia treatment in Bangalore, you don’t have to put up with it.

When is it anemia?

According to the World Health Organization, you have anemia if your hemoglobin is below:

WhoHemoglobin below
Adult men13 g/dL
Adult women (not pregnant)12 g/dL
Pregnant women11 g/dL
Children (6 months to 5 years)11 g/dL

Anemia can be mild, moderate or severe. Even mild anemia is worth checking, because it is often an early sign of another problem.

Signs & symptoms

Common signs of anemia

Anemia often develops slowly, so many people don’t notice it at first. Watch for:

  • Constant tirednessFeeling tired or weak all the time
  • PalenessPale skin, lips or inner eyelids
  • BreathlessnessOn stairs or while walking
  • Fast heartbeatA fast or uneven heartbeat
  • DizzinessHeadaches or feeling light-headed
  • Cold hands & feetEven in normal weather
  • Brittle nails, hair fallWeak nails and thinning hair
  • Poor focusTrouble concentrating at work or school
  • Unusual cravingsIce, mud or chalk — a sign of low iron

Anemia symptoms in women

Women are more likely to have anemia because of monthly periods, pregnancy and breastfeeding. Heavy periods, constant tiredness and hair fall are common clues.

Anemia symptoms in children

Children may look pale, eat poorly, get irritable, fall sick often or grow more slowly. Dr. Neema Bhat is a US board-certified pediatrician and pediatric hematologist, so children with anemia get specialised care.

See a doctor urgently if you have chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, or black or bloody stools.

Causes

What causes anemia?

Anemia is not one disease. It is a sign that something is wrong. The common causes are:

  • Low iron, vitamin B12 or folate

    From diet, poor absorption, or vegetarian diets low in B12.

  • Blood loss

    Heavy periods, piles, stomach ulcers, or slow bleeding in the gut.

  • Pregnancy

    The growing baby needs extra iron.

  • Long-term illnesses

    Kidney disease, thyroid problems, diabetes, arthritis or infections.

  • Inherited blood conditions

    Thalassemia and sickle cell disease.

  • Red cells breaking down early

    Called hemolytic anemia.

  • Bone marrow problems

    Aplastic anemia, or blood cancers like leukemia and myeloma.

  • Certain medicines

    Some long-term medicines or chemotherapy.

Who is at risk of anemia?

Anyone can get anemia, but the risk is higher for:

  • Women with heavy periods, and pregnant or breastfeeding women
  • Babies, young children and teenagers, who grow fast and need more iron
  • Vegetarians and vegans, who may get too little vitamin B12
  • Adults over 65
  • People with kidney disease, diabetes, thyroid problems, IBD or celiac disease
  • People who have had stomach or weight-loss surgery
  • People who take painkillers (NSAIDs), acid-reducing tablets or metformin for a long time
  • People with worm infections, which can cause slow blood loss
  • Anyone with a family history of thalassemia or sickle cell disease

What happens if anemia is left untreated?

Mild anemia may feel like simple tiredness, but over time it can cause serious problems:

  • Heart strain: the heart pumps harder to deliver oxygen, which can lead to an enlarged heart or heart failure.
  • Pregnancy problems: higher risk of preterm birth, low birth weight and heavy bleeding during delivery.
  • Slower growth in children: delayed growth, poor school performance and frequent infections.
  • Weak immunity and low energy: that affect work and daily life.
  • A missed diagnosis: anemia can be the first sign of hidden bleeding, kidney disease or a blood cancer.

Types

Types of anemia we treat

Iron deficiency anemia

The most common type. The body lacks iron to make hemoglobin.

Vitamin B12 & folate deficiency

Megaloblastic and pernicious anemia. Low B12 or folate makes red cells large and weak. Common in vegetarians.

Anemia of chronic disease

Caused by long-term kidney, liver or inflammatory illness.

Hemolytic anemia

Including autoimmune. Red cells are destroyed faster than the body can replace them.

Thalassemia

An inherited condition where the body makes faulty hemoglobin.

Sickle cell anemia

Inherited; red cells become sickle-shaped and block blood flow.

Aplastic anemia

The bone marrow stops making enough blood cells.

Sideroblastic anemia

The body cannot use iron properly to make hemoglobin.

Anemia in pregnancy & children

Special care for mothers-to-be, babies and teenagers.

Diagnosis

How is anemia diagnosed?

Finding the right cause is the most important step. Dr. Neema Bhat may advise some of these anemia blood tests:

  1. 01
    Complete Blood Count (CBC)

    Checks hemoglobin and the size and number of red cells.

  2. 02
    Peripheral blood smear

    Looks at your blood cells under a microscope.

  3. 03
    Iron studies

    Serum iron, ferritin and TIBC to measure iron stores.

  4. 04
    Vitamin B12 and folate levels

    To find vitamin deficiencies.

  5. 05
    Reticulocyte count

    Shows whether your bone marrow is making new cells.

  6. 06
    Hemoglobin electrophoresis / HPLC

    To check for thalassemia or sickle cell.

  7. 07
    Kidney, liver and thyroid tests

    To look for long-term illnesses behind the anemia.

  8. 08
    Stool test or endoscopy

    If hidden bleeding is suspected.

  9. 09
    Bone marrow test

    Only when needed, for aplastic anemia or blood cancers.

Treatment

Anemia treatment options in Bangalore

Your anemia treatment in Bangalore depends on the type and cause of your condition. Dr. Neema Bhat creates a plan for you that may include:

Iron tablets or syrup

The usual iron deficiency anemia treatment for mild to moderate cases.

IV iron infusion

A faster option when tablets cause stomach upset, don’t work, or iron is needed quickly — for example in pregnancy or before surgery.

Vitamin B12 & folic acid

B12 injections or tablets and folic acid for B12 and folate deficiency.

Treating the root cause

Such as heavy periods, gut bleeding, thyroid or kidney problems, with the right specialist.

Immune-calming medicines

For autoimmune hemolytic anemia.

Blood transfusion

Only for severe anemia or emergencies.

Transfusion & iron chelation

Regular transfusions with iron chelation for thalassemia.

Immunosuppressive therapy

For aplastic anemia.

Bone marrow (stem cell) transplant

A possible cure for thalassemia, sickle cell disease and severe aplastic anemia. Learn more

Diet & lifestyle advice

Simple Indian food choices to keep your hemoglobin healthy.

Do not start iron tablets on your own for a long time. Too much iron can harm the body, and the tablets may hide a more serious cause.

Foods that help treat and prevent anemia

Food supports treatment but rarely fixes anemia alone. Include:

  • Iron-rich foods: ragi, green leafy vegetables (palak, methi, drumstick leaves), dals, rajma, chana, jaggery, dates, eggs, chicken, fish and meat.
  • Vitamin C with meals: lemon, amla, orange or guava help your body absorb iron.
  • Vitamin B12 sources: milk, curd, paneer, eggs and fish.
  • Avoid tea or coffee within one hour of meals: it reduces iron absorption.

Prevention & carrier testing

India’s national Anemia Mukt Bharat programme gives free iron and folic acid tablets to children, teenagers, and pregnant and breastfeeding women through government health centres.

Inherited anemias such as thalassemia cannot be prevented with food. A simple thalassemia carrier test (HPLC) before marriage or pregnancy shows whether a couple could pass on the condition. If both partners are carriers, each child has a 1 in 4 chance of thalassemia major, so early counselling helps families plan.

01Which doctor should I see for anemia in Bangalore?

A hematologist (blood specialist) is the right doctor for anemia that is moderate, severe, keeps coming back, or does not improve with iron tablets. Dr. Neema Bhat is a US-trained hematologist in Bangalore who treats all types of anemia in adults and children.

02What hemoglobin level is considered anemia?

The WHO defines anemia as hemoglobin below 13 g/dL in men, below 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women, and below 11 g/dL in pregnant women and young children.

03Can anemia be cured permanently?

Yes, most anemias caused by low iron, B12 or folate can be cured once the cause is corrected. Inherited types like thalassemia need long-term care, and some can be cured with a bone marrow transplant.

04How long does anemia treatment take?

Most people with iron deficiency feel better in 2 to 3 weeks, and hemoglobin returns to normal in about 2 months. Treatment usually continues for a few more months to rebuild iron stores.

05What is the fastest way to treat anemia?

The fastest safe treatment depends on the cause. For iron deficiency, an IV iron infusion can raise iron levels much faster than tablets. Blood transfusion is used only for severe anemia or emergencies.

06Can anemia be treated without a blood transfusion?

Yes. Most patients are treated with iron tablets, IV iron, vitamin B12 or folic acid, and by fixing the root cause. Transfusions are needed only in severe or special cases.

07Does seeing a hemato-oncologist for anemia mean I have cancer?

No. A hemato-oncologist treats all blood problems: common ones like anemia as well as blood cancers. Most anemia is caused by low iron or vitamins. Seeing a blood specialist simply means the cause is found faster and treated correctly.

08Can anemia come back after treatment?

It can, if the underlying cause, such as heavy periods, a poor diet or slow bleeding in the gut, is not fixed. Regular follow-up blood tests help prevent this.

09How much does anemia treatment in Bangalore cost?

Most anemia treatment, such as blood tests and iron or vitamin supplements, is affordable. Costs rise only for IV infusions, transfusions or specialised treatments. The clinic can share an estimate after your first consultation.

10Can children and pregnant women get anemia treatment?

Yes. Dr. Neema Bhat is a US board-certified pediatrician and hematologist, and she treats anemia in babies, children, teenagers and pregnant women with safe, age-appropriate care.
